**House of Hades** is the dark realm of the dead beneath the earth, ruled by Hades and Proserpine, to which the souls of mortals descend when they die.

## Appearances

The house of Hades is referenced throughout the Iliad (from Book I onward, e.g. Books I, XV, and XX) as the destination of slain warriors' souls. In the Odyssey it is the setting of Ulysses' visit to the dead in Book XI (the Nekuia), with the approach described by Circe in Book X.

## Significance

- It is the portion of the world that fell by lot to Hades when the three sons of Saturn divided creation: "We were three brothers whom Rhea bore to Saturn- Jove, myself, and Hades who rules the world below... Hades took the darkness of the realms under the earth" (Iliad, Book XV).
- It lies beyond the stream of Oceanus, near the meeting of the underworld's rivers: "here beach your ship upon the shore of Oceanus, and go straight on to the dark abode of Hades. You will find it near the place where the rivers Pyriphlegethon and Cocytus (which is a branch of the river Styx) flow into Acheron" (Odyssey, Book X).
- Its inhabitants are insubstantial phantoms who cannot be embraced by the living: "Thrice I sprang towards her and tried to clasp her in my arms, but each time she flitted from my embrace as it were a dream or phantom... we might find sad comfort in the sharing of our sorrows even in the house of Hades" (Odyssey, Book XI).

## Inhabitants

- Hades, who rules the world below and to whom Ulysses is bidden to make prayer and sacrifice.
- Proserpine (Persephone), the dread queen of the dead who reigns alongside Hades.
- Minos, son of Jove, who sits in judgement over the dead: "the ghosts were gathered sitting and standing round him in the spacious house of Hades, to learn his sentences upon them" (Odyssey, Book XI).
