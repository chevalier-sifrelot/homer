**Pylos** is the coastal city ruled by the aged knight Nestor, sender of ships to Troy and the first stop on Telemachus' search for news of his father.

## Appearances

Pylos appears in the Iliad (Books I, II, and XI among others) and prominently in the Odyssey (Books I, III, and others), where it is the destination of Telemachus' first journey.

## Significance

- Pylos was ruled for generations by Nestor, its wise and long-lived king: "Two generations of men born and bred in Pylos had passed away under his rule, and he was now reigning over the third." (Iliad, Book I)
- It contributed a large contingent to the war against Troy: "These were commanded by Nestor, knight of Gerene, and with him there came ninety ships." (Iliad, Book II)
- It is the city of Neleus, where Telemachus lands to find its people at sacrifice: "they reached Pylos the city of Neleus. Now the people of Pylos were gathered on the sea shore to offer sacrifice of black bulls to Neptune lord of the Earthquake." (Odyssey, Book III)

## Inhabitants

- Nestor, king of Pylos and its aged counsellor.
- Neleus, from whom the city descends, called "the city of Neleus" (Iliad, Book XI)
- The Pylians, its people, described as gathered on the sea shore for sacrifice.
