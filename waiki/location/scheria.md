**Scheria** is the remote island home of the Phaeacians, ruled by King Alcinous, where the shipwrecked Ulysses is welcomed and finally sped on his way home to Ithaca.

## Appearances

Scheria appears in the Odyssey, in Books VI, VII, VIII, and XIII (it is first named in Book V as Ulysses approaches it).

## Significance

- The Phaeacians were resettled on Scheria by King Nausithous to escape the Cyclopes, and it was fortified as a walled city: "king Nausithous moved them thence and settled them in Scheria, far from all other people. He surrounded the city with a wall, built houses and temples, and divided the lands among his people" (Odyssey, Book VI).
- The island lies apart from the rest of the world, its people favoured by the gods: "we are dear to the gods, and live apart on a land's end that juts into the sounding sea, and have nothing to do with any other people" (Odyssey, Book VI).
- Alcinous's palace boasts an extraordinary, ever-fruitful garden: "Outside the gate of the outer court there is a large garden of about four acres with a wall all round it. It is full of beautiful trees- pears, pomegranates, and the most delicious apples... The fruits never rot nor fail all the year round, neither winter nor summer" (Odyssey, Book VII).

## Inhabitants

- Alcinous, king of the Phaeacians, "in whom the whole power of the state is vested" (Odyssey, Book VI).
- Arete, the honoured queen and wife of Alcinous.
- Nausicaa, the daughter of King Alcinous, who discovers Ulysses on the shore.
