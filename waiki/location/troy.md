**Troy** is the great walled city of the Trojans, also called Ilius, besieged by the Achaeans throughout the war of the Iliad.

## Appearances

Troy appears throughout the Iliad, from Book I to Book XXIV, and is referred to in the Odyssey as the city from which the heroes return.

## Significance

- The city is the ultimate prize the Achaeans have come to sack, promised to them by Jove himself: "if ever Jove grants us to sack the city of Troy we will requite you three and fourfold." (Iliad, Book I)
- Its capture is bound up with the will of the gods, whose promise proves treacherous: "Cruel Jove gave me his solemn promise that I should sack the city of Troy before returning, but he has played me false, and is now bidding me go ingloriously back to Argos with the loss of much people." (Iliad, Book IX)
- Its greatest defender is its own champion, whose loss dooms the city: "Night and day did I glory in you throughout the city, for you were a tower of strength to all in Troy, and both men and women alike hailed you as a god." (Iliad, Book XXII)

## Inhabitants

- Priam, the aged king of Troy.
- Hector, the city's foremost warrior and its "tower of strength."
- Hecuba, Priam's wife, who leads the wailing of the Trojan women.
