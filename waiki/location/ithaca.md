# Ithaca

**Ithaca** is the rugged island home and kingdom of Ulysses, to which he longs to return throughout the Odyssey.

## Appearances

Ithaca appears throughout the Odyssey, from Book I onward, and is the destination of Ulysses' long homeward journey.

## Significance

- It is a rugged, mountainous island lying furthest out to sea, yet its lord loves it above all places: "I live in Ithaca, where there is a high mountain called Neritum, covered with forests... It is a rugged island, but it breeds brave men, and my eyes know none that they better love to look upon." (Odyssey, Book IX)
- Its terrain is poor for horses and better suited to goats, a fact its ruler embraces: "in Ithaca we have neither open fields nor racecourses, and the country is more fit for goats than horses, and I like it the better for that... Ithaca least of all." (Odyssey, Book IV)
- It is the home from which Ulysses is kept away against his will while a captive: "The third man,' he answered, 'is Ulysses who dwells in Ithaca. I can see him in an island sorrowing bitterly in the house of the nymph Calypso, who is keeping him prisoner." (Odyssey, Book IV)

## Inhabitants

- Ulysses (Odysseus), the island's king.
- Telemachus, son of Ulysses.
- Laertes, father of Ulysses, who lives in Ithaca.
