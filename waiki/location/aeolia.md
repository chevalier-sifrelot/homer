**Aeolia** is the floating island of Aeolus, keeper of the winds, where Ulysses was received hospitably and given the winds bound in a sack.

## Appearances

Aeolia appears in the Odyssey, in Book X, both when Ulysses first arrives and is given the winds, and when he is driven back to the island a second time.

## Significance

- The island floats upon the sea and is ringed by an unbroken wall: "It is an island that floats (as it were) upon the sea, iron bound with a wall that girds it." (Odyssey, Book X)
- Its ruler Aeolus gave Ulysses the winds sealed in an ox-hide, having been made master of them by Jove: "he flayed me a prime ox-hide to hold the ways of the roaring winds, which he shut up in the hide as in a sack- for Jove had made him captain over the winds, and he could stir or still each one of them according to his own pleasure." (Odyssey, Book X)
- When Ulysses returned a second time, Aeolus drove him from the island as one accursed: "Vilest of mankind, get you gone at once out of the island; him whom heaven hates will I in no wise help." (Odyssey, Book X)

## Inhabitants

- Aeolus, son of Hippotas, dear to the immortal gods, who rules the island and commands the winds.
- The six sons of Aeolus, who live with their father feasting and enjoying every kind of luxury.
- The six daughters of Aeolus, whom he married to his sons so that all dwell together in his house.
