**Sparta** is the city of Menelaus (also called Lacedaemon), home of Helen before her flight to Troy and a destination in Telemachus' quest for his father.

## Appearances

Sparta, also called Lacedaemon, appears in the Iliad (Books II and III) and in the Odyssey (Books I, III, IV, and XV), where Telemachus visits Menelaus there.

## Significance

- Sparta and its surrounding land were led to Troy by Menelaus: "these were led by Menelaus of the loud battle-cry, brother to Agamemnon, and of them there were sixty ships." (Iliad, Book II)
- It was Helen's home, from which Paris carried her off, sparking the war: "not even when I first carried you off from Lacedaemon and sailed away with you" (Iliad, Book III)
- It is the seat of Menelaus' household, where Telemachus finds him at a wedding feast: "They reached the low lying city of Lacedaemon them where they drove straight to the of abode Menelaus" (Odyssey, Book IV)

## Inhabitants

- Menelaus, king of Sparta and leader of its ships to Troy.
- Helen, whose home it was before she was carried off to Troy.
- Megapenthes, Menelaus' son, for whom "he had found a bride from Sparta."
