**Aeaea** is the wooded island home of the goddess Circe, where Ulysses and his men landed on their wanderings and were detained by her enchantments.

## Appearances

Aeaea appears in the Odyssey, in Book X (Ulysses' arrival and his men's transformation) and Book XII (his return to the island after visiting the house of Hades).

## Significance

- The island is the dwelling of the cunning goddess Circe, daughter of the sun, whose house lies hidden in a forest: "had lost our comrades, and came to the Aeaean island, where Circe lives a great and cunning goddess who is own sister to the magician Aeetes- for they are both children of the sun by Perse, who is daughter to Oceanus." (Odyssey, Book X)
- Wild beasts, tamed by sorcery, prowl around Circe's house on the island: "There were wild mountain wolves and lions prowling all round it- poor bewitched creatures whom she had tamed by her enchantments and drugged into subjection." (Odyssey, Book X)
- On Aeaea Circe drugged and transformed Ulysses' men into swine: "she drugged it with wicked poisons to make them forget their homes, and when they had drunk she turned them into pigs by a stroke of her wand, and shut them up in her pigsties." (Odyssey, Book X)

## Inhabitants

- Circe, the great and cunning goddess who rules the island and works her enchantments there.
- Eurylochus, the captain who led a company to Circe's house but stayed outside, suspecting mischief.
- Elpenor, one of Ulysses' men, whose body was fetched from Circe's house for funeral rites when they returned to the island.
