# Antilochus

**Antilochus** is a young Pylian warrior, the son of Nestor, prized for his speed and bravery, who becomes especially close to Achilles and is the one who breaks the news of Patroclus's death to him.

## Appearances

Antilochus appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book IV, Book V, Book VI, Book XIII (where he avenges the death of a comrade), Book XV, Book XVI, Book XVII, Book XVIII (where he brings Achilles the news of Patroclus's death), and Book XXIII (where he competes in the chariot race and foot race of the funeral games).

## Personality

- **Compassionate and devoted**: he comforts the grief-stricken Achilles after delivering the terrible news of Patroclus's death — "Antilochus bent over him the while, weeping and holding both his hands as he lay groaning for he feared that he might plunge a knife into his own throat" (Iliad, Book XVIII).
- **Fierce yet loyal in battle**: his grief for a fallen comrade only sharpens his resolve rather than causing him to abandon his duty — "Noble Antilochus was more angry than any one, but grief did not make him forget his friend and comrade. He ran up to him, bestrode him, and covered him with his shield" (Iliad, Book XIII).
- **Respectful toward his elders despite his competitive spirit**: after driving recklessly to win the chariot race, he humbly submits to Menelaus — "Forgive me; I am much younger, King Menelaus, than you are; you stand higher than I do and are the better man of the two" (Iliad, Book XXIII).
