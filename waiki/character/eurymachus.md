# Eurymachus

**Eurymachus**, son of Polybus, is one of the leading suitors of Penelope alongside Antinous, outwardly smooth-tongued but secretly as dangerous and self-serving as his companion.

## Appearances

Eurymachus appears only in the Odyssey, playing a prominent role in Books I, II, XVI, XVIII, XX, and XXII, where he is killed.

## Personality

- **Two-faced and deceitful**: he reassures Penelope that Telemachus is safe, but the narrator reveals "he said this to quiet her, but in reality he was plotting against Telemachus" (Odyssey, Book XVI).
- **Scornful and dismissive**: he mocks the old prophet Halitherses, telling him "Go home, old man, and prophesy to your own children, or it may be worse for them" (Odyssey, Book II).
- **Quick to violent temper**: humiliated by the disguised Ulysses' words, "Eurymachus was furious at all this. He scowled at him and cried, 'You wretch, I will soon pay you out for daring to say such things to me, and in public too'" (Odyssey, Book XVIII).

## Entourage

- Antinous
