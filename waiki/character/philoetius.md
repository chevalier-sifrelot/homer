# Philoetius

**Philoetius** is the loyal cowherd (stockman) of Odysseus's household, who remains faithful to his absent master and fights alongside him and Eumaeus to destroy the suitors.

## Appearances

Philoetius appears in the Odyssey, playing a notable role in Books XX, XXI, XXII, XXIII, and XXIV.

## Personality

- **Fiercely loyal to his absent master**: when asked whether he would side with Ulysses or the suitors, he declares, "Father Jove... would indeed that you might so ordain it. If some god were but to bring Ulysses back, you should see with what might and main I would fight for him" (Odyssey, Book XXI).
- **Compassionate and easily moved**: seeing his master's old bow brought out for the contest, "the stockman wept also when he saw his master's bow" (Odyssey, Book XXI).
- **Trustworthy and obedient**: Odysseus relies on him for a critical task, charging him, "I charge you, Philoetius, to make fast the doors of the outer court, and to bind them securely at once," and "Philoetius slipped quietly out and made fast the gates" (Odyssey, Book XXI).
