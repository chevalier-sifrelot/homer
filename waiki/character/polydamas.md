# Polydamas

**Polydamas** is a Trojan captain and close comrade of Hector, born on the same night as him, whose cautious and prescient counsel is repeatedly overruled with disastrous consequences for Troy.

## Appearances

Polydamas appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book XII (reading the omen of the eagle and the serpent before the wall), Book XIII, and Book XVIII (urging the Trojans to retreat within the city before Achilles returns to battle).

## Personality

- **A man of rare judgment and foresight**: the narrator singles him out among the Trojan captains for his wisdom — "Polydamas son of Panthous was first to speak, a man of judgement, who alone among them could look both before and after" (Iliad, Book XVIII).
- **Cautious and attentive to omens others dismiss**: he warns the Trojans against pressing their luck at the wall — "the eagle let go her hold; she did not succeed in taking it home to her little ones, and so will it be- with ourselves" (Iliad, Book XII).
- **Persistently ignored despite being right**: his sound advice is repeatedly set aside in favour of Hector's boldness — "the wise words of Polydamas no man would heed" (Iliad, Book XVIII).
