# Teiresias

| | |
| --- | --- |

| **Origin** | Thebes |
| **Gender** | Male |

**Teiresias** is the blind Theban prophet whose ghost Odysseus consults in the underworld to learn how he may finally reach home; he foretells the dangers of the Thrinacian cattle, the suitors, and Odysseus's eventual peaceful death. As a shade encountered beyond the world of the living, he belongs to neither the Achaean nor Trojan camps.

## Appearances

Teiresias appears in the Odyssey in Book XI, where Odysseus travels to the house of Hades specifically to consult him, and his prophecy is recalled again in Books XII and XXIII.

## Personality

- **Wise and solemnly authoritative**: he comes forward "with his golden sceptre in his hand. He knew me and said, 'Ulysses, noble son of Laertes, why, poor man, have you left the light of day and come down to visit the dead in this sad place?'" (Odyssey, Book XI).
- **Clear-sighted about fate despite his blindness**: "'You want to know,' said he, 'about your return home, but heaven will make this hard for you. I do not think that you will escape the eye of Neptune'" (Odyssey, Book XI).
- **Precise and practical in his counsel**: he warns, "If you leave these flocks unharmed and think of nothing but of getting home, you may yet after much hardship reach Ithaca; but if you harm them, then I forewarn you of the destruction both of your ship and of your men" (Odyssey, Book XI).

## Entourage

- Odysseus
- Anticlea, Odysseus's mother, whose ghost he speaks with just after Teiresias
- Poseidon (Neptune), whose anger Teiresias warns Odysseus about
