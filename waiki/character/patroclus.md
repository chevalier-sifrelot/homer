# Patroclus

| **Origin** | Opus |

**Patroclus** is Achilles's closest companion, a gentle and loyal warrior whose death at Hector's hands, while wearing Achilles's armor, drives Achilles back into the war and toward his own doom.

## Appearances

He has a major role in the Iliad, especially in Books IX, XI, XVI (his death), XVII (the battle over his body), XVIII, XIX, and XXIII (his funeral games). He is briefly recalled in the Odyssey in Books III, XI, and XXIV.

## Personality

- **Kind and courteous**: Menelaus urges the Achaeans to defend his corpse, saying, "now indeed remember how good Patroclus was; he was ever courteous while alive, bear it in mind now that he is dead" (Iliad, Book XVII).
- **Loyal and obedient to Achilles**: when asked to run an errand, "Patroclus did as his dear comrade had bidden him, and set off running by the ships and tents of the Achaeans" (Iliad, Book XI).
- **Deeply compassionate**: he weeps before Achilles over the army's suffering, explaining, "I weep for the disaster that has now befallen the Argives" (Iliad, Book XVI).
