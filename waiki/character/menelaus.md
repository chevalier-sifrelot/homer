# Menelaus

**Menelaus** is the king of Sparta, brother of Agamemnon and husband of Helen, whose abduction by Paris is the cause of the Trojan War; he fights among the foremost Achaean chieftains and later hosts Telemachus in the Odyssey.

## Appearances

In the Iliad he plays a notable role in Books III (the duel with Paris), IV (wounded by Pandarus's arrow), XVII (defending the body of Patroclus), and XXIII. In the Odyssey he is a major figure in Book IV, and is also mentioned in Books I, III, XI, and XV.

## Personality

- **Brave in battle**: he is consistently called "brave Menelaus," as when he is struck by an arrow yet holds his ground until certain of the wound's severity (Iliad, Book IV).
- **Eager for vengeance and justice**: catching sight of Paris before their duel, "Menelaus was glad as a hungry lion... for he deemed that now he should be revenged" (Iliad, Book III).
- **A gracious and generous host**: when his servant hesitates to welcome strangers, an angry Menelaus orders, "Take their horses out, of course, and show the strangers in that they may have supper" (Odyssey, Book IV).
