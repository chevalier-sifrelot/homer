# Elpenor

**Elpenor** is the youngest and least remarkable of Odysseus's crewmen, who dies falling from Circe's roof and later becomes the first shade Odysseus meets in the underworld, begging for a proper burial.

## Appearances

Elpenor appears in the Odyssey in Book X (his drunken death on Circe's island), Book XI (his ghost's plea to Odysseus in the house of Hades), and Book XII (the crew's return to bury him).

## Personality

- **Reckless and unremarkable, undone by his own weakness**: he is introduced as "a certain youth named Elpenor, not very remarkable for sense or courage, who had got drunk and was lying on the house-top away from the rest of the men" (Odyssey, Book X).
- **Honest about his own folly**: from the underworld he freely admits his death was his own fault — "'it was all bad luck, and my own unspeakable drunkenness'" (Odyssey, Book XI).
- **Deeply anxious for proper burial and remembrance**: he pleads urgently with Odysseus, "do not go thence leaving me unwaked and unburied behind you, or I may bring heaven's anger upon you; but burn me with whatever armour I have, build a barrow for me on the sea shore" (Odyssey, Book XI).
