# Diomedes

| | |
| --- | --- |

| **Origin** | Argos |
| **Gender** | Male |

**Diomedes** (called "Diomed" in this translation) is the young king of Argos and one of the bravest Achaean warriors at Troy, celebrated for his aristeia in Book V, where he wounds even gods, and for his boldness in council and in the night raid of Book X.

## Appearances

He has a major role in the Iliad, especially in Books V (his aristeia), VIII, IX (rebuking Agamemnon), X (the night raid with Ulysses), and XXIII. He is mentioned briefly in the Odyssey in Books III and IV as one of the Achaean chiefs who returned home safely.

## Personality

- **Valorous and glory-seeking**: Minerva "put valour into the heart of Diomed, son of Tydeus, that he might excel all the other Argives, and cover himself with glory" (Iliad, Book V).
- **Bold and eager for danger**: when volunteers are needed to spy on the Trojan camp, he declares, "gladly will I visit the host of the Trojans over against us" (Iliad, Book X).
- **Outspoken, even toward his king**: he tells Agamemnon plainly, "Son of Atreus, I will chide your folly, as is my right in council" (Iliad, Book IX).
