# Nestor

**Nestor** is the aged king of Pylos, the oldest and most experienced of the Achaean leaders at Troy, revered for his wisdom and his lengthy, persuasive counsel to kings and warriors alike.

## Appearances

He plays a notable role throughout the Iliad, especially in Books I, II, VIII, X, XI, and XXIII. In the Odyssey he hosts Telemachus in Book III and is also mentioned in Books I, XI, XV, XVII, and XXIV.

## Personality

- **Eloquent and persuasive**: when he rises to calm the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles, "uprose smooth-tongued Nestor, the facile speaker of the Pylians, and the words fell from his lips sweeter than honey" (Iliad, Book I).
- **Venerably aged and experienced**: "Two generations of men born and bred in Pylos had passed away under his rule, and he was now reigning over the third" (Iliad, Book I).
- **Honest and trustworthy**: Minerva sends Telemachus to him assuring, "he will tell no lies, for he is an excellent person" (Odyssey, Book III).
