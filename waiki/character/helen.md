# Helen

**Helen** is the famously beautiful wife of Menelaus of Sparta, whose abduction by Paris (Alexandrus) sparked the Trojan War; though Greek-born, she lives in Troy for the duration of the conflict as the wife of Alexandrus, and afterward returns to Sparta with Menelaus.

## Appearances

Helen appears in the Iliad, notably in Books III, VI, VII, and XXIV, and in the Odyssey, notably in Books IV, XV, and XXIII, as the restored wife of Menelaus at Sparta.

## Personality

- **Wracked with guilt and self-reproach**: watching the fighting from Troy's walls and later mourning Hector, she calls herself "to my abhorred and sinful self" (Iliad, Book VI) and elsewhere speaks of the war fought "on account of my most shameless self" (Odyssey, Book IV).
- **Perceptive and quick-witted**: at Sparta she reads an omen before her husband can answer, saying "I will read this matter as heaven has put it in my heart, and as I doubt not that it will come to pass" (Odyssey, Book XV).
- **Gracious and kind despite her past**: Hector's family holds no grudge against her, and Helen recalls "I have never heard one word of insult or unkindness from you," weeping "for you and for my unhappy self" (Iliad, Book XXIV).

## Entourage

- Menelaus
