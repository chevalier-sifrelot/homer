# Arete

**Arete** is the queen of the Phaeacians, wife of Alcinous, honored above all women of Scheria for her wisdom, and the first person Odysseus supplicates upon reaching the Phaeacian palace. Like her husband, she belongs to neither the Achaean nor Trojan camp.

## Appearances

Arete appears in the Odyssey, playing a notable role in Books VII, VIII, XI, and XIII.

## Personality

- **The object of Odysseus's supplication**: he approaches her first upon entering the palace, pleading, "Queen Arete, daughter of great Rhexenor, in my distress I humbly pray you... to help me home to my own country as soon as possible" (Odyssey, Book VII).
- **Perceptive and admiring of worth in others**: she remarks approvingly of Odysseus to her court, asking "What do you think of this man, O Phaecians? Is he not tall and good looking, and is he not Clever?" (Odyssey, Book XI).
- **Practically caring toward guests**: she personally arranges for Odysseus's comfort, and it is said that "Then Arete told her maids to set a bed in the room" for him (Odyssey, Book VII).
