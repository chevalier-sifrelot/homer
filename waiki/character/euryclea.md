# Euryclea

**Euryclea** is the elderly and devoted nurse of Odysseus and Telemachus, a trusted housekeeper in the palace of Ithaca who recognizes the disguised Odysseus by the scar on his leg and keeps his secret until the suitors are destroyed.

## Appearances

Euryclea appears in the Odyssey, playing a notable role in Books I, II, IV, XVII, XIX, XX, XXI, and XXIII.

## Personality

- **Loyal and discreet**: she confesses to hiding Telemachus's secret voyage rather than betray his trust, telling Penelope "You may kill me, Madam, or let me live on in your house, whichever you please, but I will tell you the real truth" (Odyssey, Book IV).
- **Honest and observant**: when asked which maidservants had been disloyal, she answers plainly, "I will tell you the truth, my son... Of these, twelve in all have misbehaved, and have been wanting in respect to me, and also to Penelope" (Odyssey, Book XXII).
- **Devoted to her masters**: overjoyed at Odysseus's return, she insists to a disbelieving Penelope, "I am not mocking you. It is quite true as I tell you that Ulysses is come home again" (Odyssey, Book XXIII).

## Entourage

- Penelope
