# Thersites

**Thersites** is a lowborn, ugly common soldier in the Achaean army, notorious for loudly and abusively criticizing the Achaean leaders, especially Agamemnon, until Odysseus publicly beats and humiliates him into silence.

## Appearances

Thersites appears only in Book II of the Iliad, in the assembly scene where he rails against Agamemnon and is struck down by Odysseus.

## Personality

- **Unrestrained and quarrelsome**: he cannot stop talking even when it earns him hatred and scorn — "Thersites still went on wagging his unbridled tongue- a man of many words, and those unseemly; a monger of sedition, a railer against all who were in authority" (Iliad, Book II).
- **Physically repugnant, marking him as an outsider among the heroes**: "He was the ugliest man of all those that came before Troy- bandy-legged, lame of one foot, with his two shoulders rounded and hunched over his chest" (Iliad, Book II).
- **Bold enough to insult the highest-ranking leader**: he openly mocks Agamemnon in front of the whole army — "Agamemnon," he cried, "what ails you now, and what more do you want? Your tents are filled with bronze and with fair women" (Iliad, Book II).

## Entourage

- Agamemnon
