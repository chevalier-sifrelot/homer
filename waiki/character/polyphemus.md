# Polyphemus

**Polyphemus** is a man-eating Cyclops, son of Poseidon, who traps Odysseus and his men in his cave and devours several of them before being blinded and outwitted by Odysseus's trick with the name "Noman." He exists entirely outside the Trojan War, in the monstrous world Odysseus encounters on his journey home.

## Appearances

Polyphemus appears in the Odyssey in Book IX, where Odysseus recounts the encounter on the island of the Cyclopes, and he is referenced again in Book I as the reason for Poseidon's anger at Odysseus.

## Personality

- **Impious and contemptuous of the gods**: "Talk to me, indeed, about fearing the gods or shunning their anger? We Cyclopes do not care about Jove or any of your blessed gods, for we are ever so much stronger than they" (Odyssey, Book IX).
- **Savage and cruel**: he devours his guests, and even his fellow Cyclopes call out in alarm, "'What ails you, Polyphemus,' said they, 'that you make such a noise, breaking the stillness of the night'" (Odyssey, Book IX).
- **Easily deceived once drunk**: after being given wine, he tells Odysseus, "'Then I will eat all Noman's comrades before Noman himself, and will keep Noman for the last. This is the present that I will make him'" (Odyssey, Book IX).

## Entourage

- Odysseus
- Poseidon (Neptune), his father
- The other Cyclopes who live on the "bleak headlands near him" (Odyssey, Book IX)
