# Teucer

**Teucer** is the greatest archer among the Achaeans, the half-brother of Ajax the Great, who fights from behind his brother's shield and takes a heavy toll on the Trojans with his bow.

## Appearances

Teucer appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book VI, Book VIII (where he kills a string of Trojan warriors), Book XII, Book XIII, Book XIV, Book XV (where Jove breaks his bowstring just as he takes aim at Hector), Book XVI, and Book XXIII (where he competes in the archery contest at the funeral games).

## Personality

- **A deadly and disciplined archer**: he picks off Trojans one by one from behind his brother's shield — "Teucer would peer round, and when he had hit any one in the throng, the man would fall dead; then Teucer would hie back to Ajax as a child to its mother" (Iliad, Book VIII).
- **Devoted to his brother and comrades**: he rushes to avenge a fallen friend the moment he is called — Ajax cries "Teucer, my good fellow, our trusty comrade the son of Mastor has fallen... fetch your deadly arrows at once," and "Teucer heard him and hastened towards him with his bow and quiver in his hands" (Iliad, Book XV).
- **Prone to frustration when fate thwarts him**: when Jove spoils his shot at Hector, he reacts with visible anger rather than resignation — "Teucer shook with anger and said to his brother, 'Alas, see how heaven thwarts us in all we do'" (Iliad, Book XV).

## Entourage

- Ajax (son of Telamon)
- Agamemnon
- Meriones
