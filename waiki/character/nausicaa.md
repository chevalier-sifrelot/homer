# Nausicaa

**Nausicaa** is the young princess of the Phaeacians, daughter of Alcinous and Arete, who discovers the shipwrecked and destitute Odysseus on the beach and kindly guides him toward help. As a Phaeacian, she stands outside both the Achaean and Trojan camps.

## Appearances

Nausicaa appears in the Odyssey, playing a notable role in Books VI and VIII.

## Personality

- **Kind and generous to a stranger in distress**: she reassures the frightened maids and orders help for Odysseus, saying "This is only some poor man who has lost his way, and we must be kind to him, for strangers and foreigners in distress are under Jove's protection" (Odyssey, Book VI).
- **Composed and sensible**: Odysseus himself calls her "a sensible, well-disposed person," and she calmly explains her people's customs and her own status, "I am daughter to Alcinous, in whom the whole power of the state is vested" (Odyssey, Book VI).
- **Mindful of propriety and reputation**: she is careful about appearances, admitting "I am afraid of the gossip and scandal that may be set on foot against me later on; for the people here are very ill-natured" (Odyssey, Book VI).
