# Odysseus

**Odysseus** (called "Ulysses" in this translation) is the king of Ithaca renowned for his cunning intelligence, whose ten-year journey home after the fall of Troy is the subject of the Odyssey; he also plays a major counseling and fighting role in the Iliad.

## Appearances

In the Iliad he has notable roles in Books II, III, IV, IX, X, XI, XIV, and XXIII. He is the protagonist of the entire Odyssey, appearing in all twenty-four books.

## Personality

- **Cunning and resourceful**: Helen describes him to Priam as "Ulysses, a man of great craft, son of Laertes... He was born in rugged Ithaca, and excels in all manner of stratagems and subtle cunning" (Iliad, Book III).
- **A commanding, persuasive speaker**: once he begins to speak, "the words came driving from his deep chest like winter snow before the wind, then there was none to touch him" (Iliad, Book III).
- **Enduring and courageous**: Menelaus recalls, "I have never seen such another man as Ulysses. What endurance too, and what courage he displayed within the wooden horse" (Odyssey, Book IV).
