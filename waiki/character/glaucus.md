# Glaucus

**Glaucus** is a Lycian prince and the close comrade of Sarpedon, famed for exchanging armour with the Achaean Diomed after the two discover their families are bound by ancient guest-friendship.

## Appearances

Glaucus appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book VI (his meeting with Diomed and the exchange of armour), Book XII (fighting beside Sarpedon at the wall), Book XVI (Sarpedon's death), and Book XVII (rebuking Hector).

## Personality

- **Proud of his lineage and eager to live up to it**: he recalls his father's charge to always excel — "he urged me again and again to fight ever among the foremost and outvie my peers, so as not to shame the blood of my fathers who were the noblest in Ephyra and in all Lycia" (Iliad, Book VI).
- **Devoted to Sarpedon, even in helpless grief**: wounded himself, he cannot save his dying friend — "Glaucus was overcome with grief when he heard what Sarpedon said, for he could not help him" (Iliad, Book XVI).
- **Outspoken and unafraid to rebuke even Hector**: he openly criticises Hector's conduct in front of the army — "A runaway like yourself has no claim to so great a reputation" (Iliad, Book XVII).

## Entourage

- Sarpedon
- Hector
- Diomed
