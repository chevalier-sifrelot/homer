# Achilles

**Achilles** is the greatest warrior of the Achaean army before Troy, son of Peleus and the sea-goddess Thetis; his wrath at Agamemnon and his grief over the death of Patroclus drive the entire plot of the Iliad.

## Appearances

Achilles is central to the Iliad, appearing prominently in Books I, IX, XVI, XVIII, XIX, XX, XXI, XXII, XXIII, and XXIV. He is also spoken of at length in the Odyssey, notably in Book XI, where his ghost speaks with Ulysses in the underworld, and in Book XXIV.

## Personality

- **Wrathful and proud**: the epic itself opens on this trait — "Sing, O goddess, the anger of Achilles son of Peleus, that brought" countless woes upon the Achaeans (Iliad, Book I).
- **Fiercely loyal to those he loves**: on learning of Patroclus's death he cries, "my dear comrade Patroclus has fallen- he whom I valued more than all others, and loved as dearly as my own life" (Iliad, Book XVIII).
- **Capable of deep compassion beneath his rage**: when Priam begs for Hector's body, "the heart of Achilles yearned as he bethought him of his father" (Iliad, Book XXIV).

## Entourage
