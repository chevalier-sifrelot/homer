# Aeneas

**Aeneas** is a Dardanian prince, son of Anchises and the goddess Venus (Aphrodite), and one of the foremost champions of Troy, twice saved from death in battle by divine intervention because the gods have decreed that his line will endure.

## Appearances

Aeneas appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book II (leading the Dardanians), Book V (wounded before Diomed and carried off by Apollo and Venus), Book XIII, and Book XX (his duel with Achilles, from which Neptune rescues him).

## Personality

- **Proud of his divine and royal lineage**: he answers Achilles' taunts by reciting his descent — "I have noble Anchises for my father, and Venus for my mother" (Iliad, Book XX).
- **Unshaken by intimidation**: he refuses to be cowed even by Achilles himself — "Son of Peleus, think not that your words can scare me as though I were a child" (Iliad, Book XX).
- **Favoured and protected by the gods**: the gods intervene to spare him because of his piety and his destined future — "Has he not at all times offered acceptable sacrifice to the gods that dwell in heaven?... It is fated, moreover, that he should escape" (Iliad, Book XX).
