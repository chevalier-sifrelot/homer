# Idomeneus

**Idomeneus** is the king of Crete and leader of the Cretan contingent before Troy, a veteran spearman whose age does nothing to dull his ferocity in battle.

## Appearances

Idomeneus appears throughout the Iliad, with notable roles in Book II (listed among the leaders of the Achaean host), Book IV, Book VII, Book VIII, Book XI, Book XIII (his greatest aristeia, in which he kills several Trojans and duels Aeneas), Book XVII, and Book XXIII (where he disputes the outcome of the chariot race).

## Personality

- **Battle-hardened despite his age**: though no longer young, he remains a terror on the battlefield — "And now Idomeneus, though his hair was already flecked with grey, called loud on the Danaans and spread panic among the Trojans as he leaped in among them" (Iliad, Book XIII).
- **Fearless and unyielding**: he stands his ground even against powerful opponents rather than retreating — "Idomeneus was not to be thus daunted as though he were a mere child; he held his ground as a wild boar at bay upon the mountains" (Iliad, Book XIII).
- **Generous and loyal to his companions**: he shares his spoils of war freely with his squire Meriones — "you will find one spear, or twenty if you so please, standing up against the end wall of my tent... therefore I have spears, bossed shields, helmets, and burnished corslets" (Iliad, Book XIII).

## Entourage

- Meriones
- Nestor
- Ajax (son of Oileus)
