# Hector

**Hector** is the eldest son of King Priam and the greatest of the Trojan warriors, commander of the Trojan army and the city's staunchest defender until his death at the hands of Achilles.

## Appearances

Hector appears throughout the Iliad, with notable roles in Book III (halting the duel between Paris and Menelaus), Book VI (his farewell to Andromache and Astyanax), Book VII (his duel with Ajax), Book VIII, Book XII (leading the assault on the Achaean wall), Book XV, Book XVI (killing Patroclus), Book XVII, Book XX, Book XXII (his death at the hands of Achilles), and Book XXIV (Priam's ransom of his body).

## Personality

- **Devoted to duty and honour above his own safety**: he refuses to shirk battle even knowing what it will cost him — "I cannot do so: I know nothing save to fight bravely in the forefront of the Trojan host and win renown alike for my father and myself" (Iliad, Book VI).
- **A tender and loving father**: even amid the horrors of war he prays for his infant son's future — "Jove, grant that this my child may be even as myself, chief among the Trojans... and let his mother's heart be glad" (Iliad, Book VI).
- **Proud and stubborn in his own judgment**: he dismisses Polydamas's cautious counsel and trusts instead in his own resolve — "Polydamas, I like not of your reading... There is one omen, and one only- that a man should fight for his country" (Iliad, Book XII).
