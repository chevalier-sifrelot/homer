**Andromache** is the wife of Hector and mother of Astyanax, a Cilician princess whose entire birth family was killed by Achilles before the war and who is left widowed and afraid for her son when Hector falls.

## Appearances

Andromache appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book VI (her farewell scene with Hector on the walls of Troy), Book XXII (her lament on learning of Hector's death), and Book XXIV (mourning over his ransomed body).

## Personality

- **Devoted and pleading, fearing for her whole family**: she begs Hector to fight defensively rather than risk leaving her a widow — "Hector- you who to me are father, mother, brother, and dear husband- have mercy upon me; stay here upon this wall" (Iliad, Book VI).
- **Overcome with grief at Hector's death**: the news strikes her like madness — "she flew from the house like a maniac, with her waiting-women following after" (Iliad, Book XXII).
- **Fearful for her son's future without a father**: she foresees a life of hardship for Astyanax now that Hector is gone — "his life henceforth be one of labour and sorrow, for others will seize his lands" (Iliad, Book XXII).
