# Priam

**Priam** is the aged king of Troy, father of Hector, Paris, and many other sons and daughters, whose reign is defined by the long war fought over his son's theft of Helen and by the grief of losing his bravest child.

## Appearances

Priam appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book III (watching the duel of Paris and Menelaus from the walls and naming the Achaean champions with Helen), Book VII, Book XXII (mourning Hector's death from the walls), and Book XXIV (his night journey to Achilles' tent to ransom Hector's body).

## Personality

- **Wise and free of bitterness**: he refuses to blame Helen for the war that has consumed his sons — "I lay no blame upon you, it is the gods, not you who are to blame" (Iliad, Book III).
- **Courageous even in his old age and grief**: he steels himself to face his son's killer in order to bring the body home — "I have steeled myself as no man yet has ever steeled himself before me, and have raised to my lips the hand of him who slew my son" (Iliad, Book XXIV).
- **A father consumed by grief for his dead**: he lashes out at his surviving sons in his sorrow — "worthless sons who do me shame; would that you had all been killed at the ships rather than Hector" (Iliad, Book XXIV).
