# Penelope

**Penelope** is the wife of Ulysses and queen of Ithaca, celebrated for her cunning and unwavering fidelity as she fends off a house full of suitors during her husband's twenty-year absence.

## Appearances

Penelope appears throughout the Odyssey, with especially notable roles in Books I, IV, XVII, XVIII, XIX, XXI, XXIII, and XXIV.

## Personality

- **Cunning and resourceful**: she deceives the suitors for years with her weaving trick, admitting "I have to invent stratagems in order to deceive them" (Odyssey, Book XIX).
- **Deeply sorrowful yet devoted**: hearing a stranger's tale of Ulysses, "her cheeks overflow with tears for the husband" she still mourns (Odyssey, Book XIX).
- **Renowned for her fidelity**: the ghost of Agamemnon praises Ulysses for having "a wife endowed with such rare excellence of understanding, and so faithful to her wedded lord as Penelope" (Odyssey, Book XXIV).
