# Laertes

**Laertes** is the elderly father of Ulysses, who has withdrawn to a life of grief-stricken farm labor during his son's long absence and is finally reunited with him near the end of the Odyssey.

## Appearances

Laertes appears only in the Odyssey, mentioned in passing in earlier books (e.g. Book XV) and playing a major role in Book XXIV, where he is reunited with Ulysses.

## Personality

- **Withdrawn into sorrow**: Eumaeus reports that "Laertes is still living and prays heaven to let him depart peacefully his own house, for he is terribly distressed about the absence of his son" (Odyssey, Book XV).
- **Cautious and hard to convince**: when Ulysses reveals himself, Laertes demands proof, replying "you must give me such manifest proof of your identity as shall convince me" (Odyssey, Book XXIV).
- **Overjoyed by his family's courage**: seeing his son and grandson fight side by side, he exclaims "what a day I am enjoying: I do indeed rejoice at it. My son and grandson are vying with one another in the matter of valour" (Odyssey, Book XXIV).
