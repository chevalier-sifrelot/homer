# Sarpedon

**Sarpedon** is a son of Jove and king of the Lycians, the foremost of Troy's allied champions, who leads the assault on the Achaean wall and is ultimately slain in single combat by Patroclus.

## Appearances

Sarpedon appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book II (leading the Lycian contingent), Book V, Book XII (his speech to Glaucus and the assault on the wall), and Book XVI (his death at the hands of Patroclus).

## Personality

- **Bound by a sense of noble obligation**: he reasons that the honours the Lycians grant him and Glaucus require them to fight at the front — "it becomes us, therefore, to take our stand at the head of all the Lycians and bear the brunt of the fight" (Iliad, Book XII).
- **Driven by a fatalistic courage**: he accepts that death cannot be escaped, so glory must be won instead — "death in ten thousand shapes hangs ever over our heads, and no man can elude him; therefore let us go forward and either win glory for ourselves, or yield it to another" (Iliad, Book XII).
- **Loyal to his comrades even as he dies**: his last words are a plea for his men and his armour, not himself — "Glaucus, my brother, hero among heroes, put forth all your strength, fight with might and main, now if ever quit yourself like a valiant soldier" (Iliad, Book XVI).
