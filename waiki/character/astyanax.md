# Astyanax

**Astyanax** is the infant son of Hector and Andromache, whose brief appearances underscore the human cost of the Trojan War and the fate that awaits Troy's royal house.

## Appearances

Astyanax appears only in the Iliad, notably in Book VI (the farewell scene between Hector and Andromache) and Book XXII (Andromache's lament foretelling his fate as an orphan).

## Personality

- **Named for his father's role as protector**: "the people called him Astyanax, for his father stood alone as chief guardian of Ilius" (Iliad, Book VI).
- **Cherished and doted upon**: as a baby he "would sit upon his father's knees, and have none but the daintiest and choicest morsels set before him" (Iliad, Book XXII).
- **Marked for a tragic future**: Andromache foresees that once orphaned "his lot will be full of hardship- he, whom the Trojans name Astyanax" (Iliad, Book XXII).
