# Meriones

**Meriones** is a Cretan warrior and the trusted squire and second-in-command of Idomeneus, a skilled fighter and archer who repeatedly proves his courage alongside his king.

## Appearances

Meriones appears throughout the Iliad, notably in Book II (catalog of ships), Book IV, Book V, Book X (the night raid with Odysseus and Diomedes), Book XIII (fighting alongside Idomeneus), Book XVI (where he trades sharp words with Aeneas), and Book XXIII (where he wins the archery contest and takes part in the chariot race).

## Personality

- **Loyal and steadfast comrade to Idomeneus**: their bond is affectionate and unwavering, as when Idomeneus greets him — "Meriones, fleet son of Molus, best of comrades, why have you left the field?" (Iliad, Book XIII).
- **Bold and unafraid to talk back to great warriors**: when mocked by Aeneas, he answers him fearlessly rather than backing down — "Aeneas, for all your bravery, you will not be able to make an end of every one who comes against you. You are only a mortal like myself" (Iliad, Book XVI).
- **A skilled and effective archer**: he outshoots even Teucer in the funeral games' archery contest — "Meriones, therefore, took all ten double-edged axes, while Teucer bore off the single-edged ones to his ships" (Iliad, Book XXIII).
