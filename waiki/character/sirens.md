# Sirens

**Sirens** are a pair of enchanting singing creatures who dwell on an island in a field of flowers, luring sailors to their doom with the irresistible beauty of their song; Odysseus survives them only by having his men bind him to the mast and plug their own ears with wax. They stand outside the Trojan War entirely, encountered purely as a hazard on Odysseus's journey home.

## Appearances

The Sirens appear in the Odyssey in Book XII, first described by Circe as a warning and then encountered directly as Odysseus sails past their island, and they are referenced again in Book XXIII.

## Personality

- **Irresistibly beautiful in song, yet deadly**: Circe warns, "if any one unwarily draws in too close and hears the singing of the Sirens, his wife and children will never welcome him home again, for they sit in a green field and warble him to death with the sweetness of their song" (Odyssey, Book XII).
- **Deceptively flattering and knowing**: "'Come here,' they sang, 'renowned Ulysses, honour to the Achaean name, and listen to our two voices... we know all the ills that the gods laid upon the Argives and Trojans before Troy'" (Odyssey, Book XII).
- **Relentless once they sense a passing ship**: "the Sirens saw that we were getting in shore and began with their singing" (Odyssey, Book XII).

## Entourage

- Odysseus
- Circe
- Odysseus's crew, including Eurylochus and Perimedes, who bind him tighter against the song
