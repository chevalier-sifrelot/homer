# Pandarus

**Pandarus** is a Trojan archer, son of Lycaon, whose arrow breaks the truce between the two armies by wounding Menelaus, and who is later killed by Diomed after failing to bring the Achaean champion down.

## Appearances

Pandarus appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book II (leading his men from Zelea), Book IV (breaking the truce by shooting Menelaus), and Book V (wounding Diomed before being killed by him).

## Personality

- **Easily persuaded into rash action**: he is talked into treachery with little resistance — "His fool's heart was persuaded, and he took his bow from its case" (Iliad, Book IV).
- **Proud of his renown as an archer**: Aeneas appeals to this pride directly before urging him to shoot Diomed — "Pandarus, where is now your bow, your winged arrows, and your renown as an archer, in respect of which no man here can rival you" (Iliad, Book V).
- **Full of regret for his own folly**: he curses the day he took up his bow against the truce — "I did ill to take my bow down from its peg on the day I led my band of Trojans to Ilius in Hector's service... may some one cut my head off then and there if I do not break the bow and set it on a hot fire" (Iliad, Book V).
