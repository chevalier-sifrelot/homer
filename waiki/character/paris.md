# Paris

**Paris** (called Alexandrus throughout Butler's translation) is the son of Priam whose abduction of Helen from Sparta caused the Trojan War, a handsome archer more comfortable with his bow and his lyre than in close combat.

## Appearances

Paris appears in several books of the Iliad, notably Book III (his duel with Menelaus, from which Aphrodite rescues him), Book VI (rebuked by Hector for lingering at home with Helen), and Book XI (wounding Diomed with an arrow).

## Personality

- **Cowardly when faced with real danger**: he flinches from single combat with the man he wronged — "Alexandrus quailed as he saw Menelaus come forward, and shrank in fear of his life under cover of his men" (Iliad, Book III).
- **Vain and more admired for looks than valour**: his own brother mocks his reputation — "evil-hearted Paris, fair to see, but woman-mad, and false of tongue" (Iliad, Book III).
- **A skilled but mocking archer who strikes from hiding**: he wounds Diomed and jeers at him from cover — "Paris with a hearty laugh sprang forward from his hiding-place, and taunted him" (Iliad, Book XI).
